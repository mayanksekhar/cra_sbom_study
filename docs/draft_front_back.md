Working title: From SBOM to finding: where correctness is lost in vulnerability handling under the Cyber Resilience Act

## Abstract
Regulation (EU) 2024/2847 makes SBOMs and vulnerability handling obligations for manufacturers of products with digital elements, and generators and scanners produce the artifacts that support them. Prior work compares generators on component accuracy. We ask whether an accurate SBOM is enough for correct vulnerability handling. For 42 project versions in five ecosystems we compare three generators (Syft, Trivy, cdxgen) against references built from each project's own package manager, score findings with Grype, use Trivy and OSV Scanner in controls, freeze the vulnerability databases, and attribute every missed finding to its cause. Of 959 reference findings (scored with Grype), no miss traces to the scanner. The losses come from the SBOM and differ by generator and ecosystem: Syft recovers 43 of 201 on Maven. Scanners read the product's version from different places, so the same SBOM gives different findings. On nine Go applications analysed at product level, product scoped VEX statements removed 319 of 319 not called findings in Trivy and 0 of 323 in Grype, which honours only module scoped statements, and a module scoped statement from one product suppressed reachable findings in another (24 of 151 called findings). A static reachability oracle for Go classifies 323 of 496 findings (65 percent) as not called at product level [RESULT PENDING: agreement of that oracle with manual labels]; a first run over whole modules overstated what is called. We map the results to the regulation and to BSI TR-03183-2 and list what an evidence grade pipeline has to declare.

## 1 Introduction
Context [V, text read]: the regulation asks manufacturers to identify and document vulnerabilities and components, including by an SBOM in a commonly used machine readable format covering at the very least the top level dependencies (Annex I Part II point 1), and to handle vulnerabilities of the product including its components effectively (Article 13(8)). Reporting of actively exploited vulnerabilities applies from 11 September 2026, the main obligations from 11 December 2027 (Article 71).
Question: is an accurate SBOM enough for correct vulnerability handling?
Approach: multi level references per ecosystem (declared, resolved, shipped, whole tree), a reference SBOM per project, the same scanner run on the reference and on each generator's SBOM, error attribution (component absent, no usable version, wrong version, scanner), controls with minimal SBOMs, and a Go reachability oracle for VEX.
Contributions, to confirm: (1) a method from SBOM to finding with miss attribution, applied to 42 project versions [V]; (2) measured failure modes: scope, lockfile dependence, version carriers, profiles, root identity [V, some on one or two projects]; (3) VEX behaviour of two scanners with controls and a cross product suppression measurement [V for Go]; (4) a mapping to the regulation and to BSI TR-03183-2 and a list of what a pipeline must declare [P, BSI from a mirror].
Structure: section 2 related work, 3 method, 4 and 5 results on SBOMs and scanners, 6 VEX and reachability, 7 what a pipeline must declare, 8 threats and limits.

## 2 Related work [O: novelty not verified, six of the papers below known from snippets only]
Generator accuracy. Mind the Gap (arXiv 2609.19920) compares Syft, Trivy and cdxgen on more than 3,000 JavaScript and Rust projects against a lockfile baseline, explains the divergences by scope, naming and provenance choices, finds empty SBOMs for Syft and Trivy without a lockfile, and covers SBOM generation only [read in full]. Python studies (arXiv 2409.06390, 2409.01214) report that generators change vulnerability scan results and identify causes of missing dependencies [snippets, O]. A study of Maven projects (Liu et al., FSE 2023) evaluates composition analysis tools on 21,130 modules [snippet, O].
SBOM based vulnerability management. A CODASPY 2026 study (arXiv 2511.20313) reports on 2,414 repositories that lockfiles give accurate, consistent SBOMs with Syft and Trivy, near perfect agreement of Grype and Trivy on the same accurate SBOM, and a Go reachability analysis that confirmed 31 of 121 reported vulnerabilities [snippets, O].
VEX and reachability. Hidden Dependencies (arXiv 2604.21278) shows on a synthetic Java benchmark around one vulnerability that Grype and CVE-Bin-Tool applied a VEX statement to no case, including the unreachable one, while Trivy applied it to all four, including three reachable ones; it measures consistency, not accuracy [read in full]. VEX-Bench (arXiv 2609.08040) reports that govulncheck completed 19 of 30 Go cases and that reachability tools do not close the gap [snippets, O]. A study of container scanners with VEX output (arXiv 2503.14388) reports low consistency among tools [snippets, O].
Position. We add, on real projects and with frozen data: findings, not only components, with every miss attributed; scanner behaviour toward version fields, profiles and file layout; VEX on real findings with a Go oracle, product versus module scope, and a cross product measurement; and a mapping to the regulation and BSI text. ## 8 Threats to validity and limits [V as statements about our work]
1. One version per tool, one snapshot (frozen 2026-10-30), Grype as the scoring scanner; OSV Scanner is not scored.
2. Popular open source projects with shared dependencies; the Go set holds 608 of 959 reference findings and PyPI findings come from two lockfile projects.
3. Reachability and VEX results are Go only; the oracle (govulncheck) is static and has no independent check yet: manual labels 0 of 40 [P].
4. Truth: Maven shipped truth has up to 3 of 237 components in doubt; PyPI install truth reflects resolution on 2026-11-07 and drifts from the pinned lock (28 of 37 poetry packages, 29 of 33 ansible-lint packages resolve to other versions).
5. Several controls use one or two projects and a minimal SBOM, so they show a mechanism, not a rate.
6. BSI text read from a mirror; the regulation read from the EUR-Lex consolidated text, which has no legal effect. No legal interpretation is offered.
7. Method errors found and corrected during the work are listed in docs/pilot_findings.md; every table is generated from files in the repository.
8. Reachability scope: the first reachability run covered whole modules and overstated what is called in the shipped binaries; the figures are now product level for nine of ten Go applications, and k9s stays at module level only because its analysis exceeded the memory available.

## 9 Discussion and future work [P]
An accurate component list is necessary but not sufficient: the identifier that carries the product's version, the graph from the product to the vulnerable component, the scope, and the form of a VEX statement each change what a scanner reports, and the three scanners read them differently. The regulation names no method for judging exploitability (Article 3(41) defines the term) and BSI names VEX and CSAF as the vehicle for the product level analysis. Future work: container images, other ecosystems at scale, other tool versions, manual validation of the reachability oracle, a controlled study of scanners that read the product's identity from a different field.
